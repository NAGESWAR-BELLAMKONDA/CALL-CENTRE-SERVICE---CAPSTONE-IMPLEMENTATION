Call-Centre Service Performance Analysis

Using an independent two-sample t-test for difference of means to compare service performance between two call-centre teams or operating conditions.

Welch's two-sample t-test Two-sided hypothesis test

1. Enter service observations

Enter numerical observations separated by commas or spaces. Use the same unit for both groups (for example, average handling time in minutes).

Illustrative synthetic sample values are preloaded for demonstration only. Replace them with verified project observations before reporting findings.

2. Hypotheses & method

Null hypothesis (H₀): μA = μB — the population mean service measure is equal for both groups.

Alternative hypothesis (H₁): μA ≠ μB — the population means differ.

t = (x̄A − x̄B) / √(sA²/nA + sB²/nB)

Welch's method does not assume equal population variances. The test is two-tailed. Observations should be independent and reasonably representative; severe outliers and strong skewness require attention.

Decision rule

Reject H₀ when p-value < α. Otherwise, fail to reject H₀. A non-significant result does not prove that the means are identical.

5. Calculation workflow

  1. Validate both samples.
  2. Calculate sample means and variances.
  3. Calculate Welch's t statistic and degrees of freedom.
  4. Find the two-tailed p-value and compare it with α.

6. Interpretation for call-centre operations

The interpretation will appear after the test. Statistical significance should be considered alongside operational impact, staffing, call complexity, customer satisfaction and service-quality measures.

This tool is an educational analysis aid, not a substitute for a documented sampling plan or operational decision review. Do not label synthetic data as actual company performance.
